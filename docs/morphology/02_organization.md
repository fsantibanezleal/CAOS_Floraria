# Floral organization and orchid differences

Read the model from outside toward its reproductive center. The general complete flower includes calyx, corolla, stamens and pistil. Follow stamen to anther/filament and pistil through stigma/style/ovary toward ovules. IDs connect structures to text and investigation states.

The orchid is not a recolored generic model. Three sepals and three petals include a differentiated lip; a reproductive column replaces the generic expectation of a free stamen ring. Inferior ovary describes insertion relative to other parts, not merely whichever object is lowest on screen.

Symmetry concerns correspondence. The general model's outer arrangement is fivefold; the whole explanatory scene need not have perfect radial symmetry in every internal structure. An oblique camera can obscure bilateral organization and a bent surface need not change the broader botanical description. No scan symmetry-classification score is calculated.

Isolation helps inspect; restoring neighbors recovers context. An exploded view removes proximity temporarily, so reassemble before interpreting attachment. Clipping is a viewing operation, not reconstruction.
